# Classroom Keepsake

Classroom Keepsake treats space as a carefully prepared teaching surface: broad calm fields hold a few tactile objects, and every object earns its place through use. Form is softened by felt edges, mounted paper, and measured seams. The final composition must feel meticulously crafted, as though every margin and join was refined by a master maker.

Colour behaves as a navigation language. Deep schoolhouse navy establishes orientation, warm ivory carries the main message, and six curriculum colours identify distinct subject pathways without competing with one another. The palette is calibrated with painstaking attention so hierarchy is understood before any label is read.

Scale follows classroom ritual. The welcome board is the visual thesis, the subject patches form a steady teaching rhythm, and the footer closes the page like a compact class register. Repetition is deliberate rather than mechanical; the master-level execution comes from consistent stitch spacing, optical alignment, and generous breathing room.

Typography is sparse and purposeful. A literary display voice gives the school warmth, a plain utility face keeps controls unmistakable, and a small handwritten accent suggests teacher annotation. Text remains essential-only, integrated into the physical composition rather than laid over it as decoration.

The subtle reference is the moment before children enter a classroom: the board is prepared, materials are sorted, and invitations to learn are already waiting. Nothing looks improvised or synthetic. The artifact should carry the quiet confidence of countless hours of careful preparation by someone at the top of their field.
