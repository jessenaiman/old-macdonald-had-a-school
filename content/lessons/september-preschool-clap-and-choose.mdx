## Purpose

Build a September participation routine in which children can hear a repeated
pattern, make a choice about how to join, and notice slow and quick movement.
The goal is shared play and listening, not a correct performance.

## Source-linked song use

Use Kathy Reid-Naiman's **Clap your Hands** from the existing linked song
record. The database record supplies the short `slowly` / `quickly` clap text
and identifies the tune as `Row, Row, Row Your Boat`. Use a permitted classroom
copy. The local baby-bounce version is a comparison source for the text and
tune note; it is not evidence that the Kathy Reid-Naiman recording is freely
available.

## Prep and materials

- Two large picture cards: `slow` and `quick`
- A clear meeting area with space to sit, stand, or work at a nearby table
- Optional rhythm tools that are safe for the group, such as two rhythm sticks or a hand drum for the teacher
- Paper strips, crayons, and stickers for the adaptable take-home card

## Brief teaching sequence

1. **Invite a choice.** Show the two cards and ask, "Would you like to clap, tap your knees, tap a table, hold a card, or listen?" Demonstrate each without requiring an answer aloud.
2. **Sing once as a model.** Keep the song short. Point to `slow` and `quick` as they occur; use the chosen participation option yourself.
3. **Let children lead a repeat.** Invite one child to hold or point to a card. Accept a gesture, look, or placement of a card as a leadership response.
4. **Open play choices.** Offer a second round with a different safe body sound or teacher-held instrument. Do not claim a new verse as source text; say the chosen action plainly before or after the documented song.
5. **Close with a routine cue.** Ask children to choose a quiet ending: hands together, hands in laps, or one last slow tap. Name the next activity.

## Participation and access choices

- Children may clap, tap, point, hum, watch, use one hand, use an assistive switch or adapted instrument, or remain at a nearby activity.
- Offer visual cards before spoken directions and give waiting time after each invitation.
- Keep sound levels adjustable; a child may participate with a soft touch or by being the card holder.
- Invite families to share a home-language word or gesture for `slow`, `quick`, `again`, or `finished` when they wish.

## Teacher observation

Notice whether the child:

- chooses or changes a participation method;
- attends when the tempo cue changes;
- repeats a body sound or gesture in their own way;
- waits for, follows, or initiates a short group turn; and
- uses `slow`, `quick`, a gesture, or another communication method to describe the experience.

## Printable/adaptable option: home-and-school tempo card

Print one strip for each child. Families can draw or attach a picture of a
preferred movement beside each cue; do not print the song lyric as a take-home
copy unless the program has permission to do so.

| Slow choice | Quick choice | My stop/finished signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Draw or place a picture | Draw or place a picture | Draw, write, or attach a picture |
