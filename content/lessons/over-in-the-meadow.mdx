## Prepare

- Editorial teacher preparation: Preview the linked video on the classroom device and confirm sound, access, and the point where you want to pause.
- Prepare number cards 1-10 and a small set of animal pictures or gestures. Use only the range that suits the group; the first version focuses on the documented 1-10 song experience.
- Arrange a seated circle or safe movement space. Fingers, a card point, or a small animal gesture can carry the activity.
- Canonical lead: Mr Sam, staff music and movement teacher. Canonical student companion: Sam.

## Watch and discuss

These are editorial prompts for using Jesse's video as a teaching model.

- Pause when a number appears: How many can we show with fingers, a card, or a quiet point?
- Pause before an animal action: Which animal do you think is next? What action could represent it?
- Pause after a counted group: How did we keep track of one animal at a time?
- Before replaying a familiar section: Should we listen for the beat, the number, or the animal action?

## Play together

This is an editorial classroom sequence built from the authoritative queue's action notes; it is not a reproduction of the song text.

1. Count together to 5 or 10, then show the first number card and its animal picture.
2. Play the video. Hold up the matching number as each verse arrives.
3. Invite children to make the animal action while keeping a gentle pat, clap, or tap on the beat.
4. Pause before a familiar number or action so children can anticipate, point, or supply the gesture.
5. End with one favourite animal action and a quiet count back to one.

## Follow up

- Editorial follow-up: Use a teacher-made count-and-circle or number-and-animal matching page.
- Invite children to choose one animal, show its action, and place its number card in sequence.
- Continue beyond the song only when it supports the group; the core music experience remains the documented 1-10 range.

## Notice

- Observable learning: The child joins the count, shows or points to a number, and matches at least one animal with an action.
- Look for one-to-one correspondence: The child attempts one gesture or finger for each counted item, even if the full range is not yet secure.
- Curriculum connection: Source mapping `CU#11` names counting to 50 and reading/writing numbers to 50 as the primary Grade 1 mapping. The direct song experience documented here is 1-10; the count-to-50 connection is an editorial extension, not a claim about the song alone and not a verified Ontario code in this record.
- Curriculum connection: Source mapping `CU#24` names representing and interpreting data with up to three categories. Sorting meadow animals by a teacher-chosen category is an editorial extension and is not required for this music page.

## Adapt

- Younger or less secure counters: use numbers 1-3 or 1-5, large cards, and one clear animal action at a time.
- Children with limited mobility: count with fingers, eye gaze, a card point, or a small hand gesture instead of whole-body animal movement.
- Sensory-sensitive children: use quiet actions and visual number cards; the child may watch or tap softly while the group sings.
- Older or ready children: continue counting beyond the song only as an editorial extension, compare one more or one less with counters, or sort animals into up to three teacher-defined groups.
