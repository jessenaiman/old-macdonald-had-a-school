## Purpose

Establish a predictable September gathering routine while children practise
hearing and representing a count sequence. The follow-up connects the shared
song to a practical classroom contribution: preparing or checking a small set
of materials for the next part of the day.

## Source-linked song use

Use Kathy Reid-Naiman's **Bell Horses** from the existing linked song record.
The database record documents a repeated `what's the time of day?` prompt and
successive number pairs from one through ten, with bells listed as the action
or instrument cue. Use a permitted classroom copy. Do not add verses,
movements, or claims about the recording that are not in the source record.

## Prep and materials

- Ten large numeral cards, 1-10
- Ten matching counters or classroom objects that can be handled safely
- A small tray or basket for the day's classroom job materials
- A bell substitute only if it is safe and available, such as a teacher-held chime; body taps are an equal option
- The teacher's permitted classroom copy of the selected song, if used

## Brief teaching sequence

1. **Welcome the group.** Review the visual schedule and name the routine: "First we count together; then we prepare our classroom materials."
2. **Use the documented count-along.** Play or sing the selected source-linked song. Invite children to show the numeral card, make a body tap, hold a counter, or listen as each number pair is heard.
3. **Represent one pair.** Stop after one documented pair. Ask children to place the matching number of counters beneath each numeral while classmates check by touching or pointing one at a time.
4. **Choose a community job.** In pairs or small groups, children count the needed items for a real next-step task: for example, cups at snack, pencils at a table, or books in a basket. Use only the quantity actually needed.
5. **Share and close.** Each group shows its counted set and says, points to, or holds up the numeral. Return materials to their place and preview the next routine.

## Participation and access choices

- A child may hold a numeral, touch each object, point while a peer counts, use an AAC response, listen for the bell/beat, or work with a partner.
- Provide objects with enough size, contrast, and spacing for the children who need them.
- Keep the song and count sequence as a group invitation; children do not need to recite aloud to belong.
- Use the child's home-language number words when families or staff supply them, and pair them with the same visible quantity.

## Teacher observation

Notice whether the child:

- connects a spoken/sung count cue to a numeral, gesture, or quantity;
- touches or moves one object at a time while counting;
- notices when a set has too many or too few objects;
- explains a count with words, pointing, marks, or AAC; and
- contributes to a shared classroom task with a peer.

## Printable/adaptable option: count-and-contribute mat

Print one reusable mat per pair. Replace the quantity and object picture to fit
the real classroom job.

| Our job | Numeral | Put this many objects here | Check together |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Draw or write the job | Attach a numeral card | Place counters/objects | Circle: `just right`, `need one more`, or `too many` |
