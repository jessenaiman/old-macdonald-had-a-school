## Lesson goal

Students use known spelling-sound patterns to blend and read one-syllable words, so the teacher can see which patterns need another brief practice cycle.

## Curriculum standards

- Ontario B2; Ontario LA B2
- US Common Core RF.1.3.b (decode regularly spelled one-syllable words)

## Source-linked song use

Use one documented version as a two-minute blending warm-up. These songs are **versions** recorded in the curriculum database; cite the source when you share them:

- **A Smooth Road** — Baby Bounce Nursery Rhymes (booklet)
- **All The Fish** — Mid North Coast Library (NSW, Australia), Baby Bounce Nursery Rhymes (booklet)
- **Animal Noises** — Mid North Coast Library (NSW, Australia), Baby Bounce Nursery Rhymes (booklet)

Use a permitted classroom copy. Do not add verses, movements, or claims about any recording that are not in the source record; say the chosen action plainly before or after the documented song text.

## Prepare

- Write 8–10 one-syllable words using only patterns already taught (for example: cat, pin, hop, mud, bed, run).
- Print or write the word list large enough for paired reading, or use your board.
- Choose your warm-up song from the list above and have your permitted copy ready.
- Keep a sticky note handy to mark words that stall the class.

## Teaching sequence

1. **Warm up with the song (3 minutes).** Sing the chosen source-linked song once. Clap or tap each beat so students feel that words come apart into sounds before you ever show print.
2. **Model successive blending (5 minutes).** Point under each spelling: /c/ /a/ /t/ → *cat*. Say the sounds, then blend once, then say the whole word naturally.
3. **Choral blend (5 minutes).** The class blends three words together while you track under the letters with a pointer.
4. **Partner decode (7 minutes).** Pairs take turns pointing and blending the remaining words. Listeners give a thumbs-up only after checking the word against the letters themselves.
5. **Record what needs another cycle (5 minutes).** Pull two or three words back up and ask individuals to blend one alone. Mark any pattern where more than a few students stall on your note — those get tomorrow's two-minute review.

## Access and observation

- Students may tap sounds on their arm, move a counter per sound, or mouth the blend quietly before saying it aloud.
- Pair students strategically so every child both blends and listens during partner decode.
- Notice which specific spelling pattern (not just which word) causes the stall — that is what goes on your reteach note.
- Graduated participation: tracking under the letters while a partner voices the blend is an active decoding role.

## Freely reachable teacher reference

- [Reading Universe](https://readinguniverse.org/) offers free, explicit blending routines and error-correction language you can use verbatim in steps 2–4.
- [UFLI Foundations](https://ufli.education.ufl.edu/foundations-toolbox/) provides free lesson slides and decodable word lists organized by the same spelling-sound patterns, so you can regenerate step 1's word list in seconds.
