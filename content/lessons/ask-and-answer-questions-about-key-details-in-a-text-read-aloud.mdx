## Lesson goal

Students listen to a short read-aloud, identify the people, actions, or facts that matter most, and support an answer by pointing back to what they heard.

## Curriculum standards

- Ontario Language 2023, C2.6 (listed in the topic record as Ontario LA C2)

## Prepare

- Choose one short read-aloud (a picture book you already own, or a free read-aloud video from [Storyline Online](https://www.storylineonline.net/)).
- Print one detective card per student (below) or draw the two columns on the board.
- Post three prompts where all students can see them: **Who was in it?** **What happened?** **How do we know?**
- Decide your evidence signal: pointing to the book page, holding up the matching picture card, or naming the part ("when the bear...").

## Teaching sequence

1. **Set the detective job (2 minutes).** Tell students their only job is to catch details that matter: who was in the story, what they did, or a fact they learned.
2. **Read once, straight through.** Enjoy the text without stopping so the story keeps its shape.
3. **Model one answer with proof (5 minutes).** Ask "Who was in it?" Answer, then show how detectives prove it: turn back to the page and point to the character.
4. **Send pairs hunting (10 minutes).** Pairs pick one prompt, find their answer, and practise showing the proof on the page or screen.
5. **Share and verify (8 minutes).** Each pair states a detail and shows the proof. The class checks by looking at the same page. Use responses to decide whether tomorrow needs another listen or can move on.

## Access and observation

- Students may answer by speaking, pointing, acting out the detail, or placing a card on the matching picture.
- Offer the exact page again for students who need memory support; returning to the text *is* the skill.
- Notice whether a student supports an answer by pointing back to what they heard, first with prompting and then independently.
- Keep participation graduated: listening with a card ready counts as joining.
