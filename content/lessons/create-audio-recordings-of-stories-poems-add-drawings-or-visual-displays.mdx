## Lesson goal

Students produce a short audio recording of a story or poem and pair it with one visual display, checking that the visual adds information for the listener.

## Prepare

- Set up one recording station per small group (tablet, computer, or voice recorder) with a 60-second limit set ahead of time.
- Copy the plan-and-check card below, one per student.
- Have paper and pencils ready; no colour printing required.

## Teaching sequence

1. **Name the communication job.** "Today we record our words AND choose a picture that helps our listener. The test is: does the picture add something the voice does not say?"
2. **Plan in 3 minutes.** Students jot (or draw) three quick notes: what happens, one feeling word, one detail worth saying. Use Think-Pair-Share from Reading Rockets to rehearse once with a partner before recording (listed topic material).
3. **Record short.** Each student records up to 60 seconds telling their story or poem. Keep takes short on purpose — done beats polished.
4. **Add the visual.** Students create ONE drawing, labelled sketch, chart, or prop photo to display beside their recording. Ask: "What does your listener SEE now that they could not hear?"
5. **Listen back and check together.** Groups play two recordings with visuals shown. Class checks each against one question only: "Did the visual add meaning?" Close by naming tomorrow's next step: revising one recording.

## Access and graduated participation

- Students may tell their story from the three-word plan, a picture sequence, or a partner's questions.
- Accept a scribed plan, an AAC-recorded narration, or a shared speaking role with a partner.
- Offer noise-reducing headphones at the station if the room is busy.
- A student who draws the visual while a partner narrates is completing half the standard — swap roles next session so both are recorded.

## Teacher observation

Notice whether students:

- stay within a short recording rather than losing the message;
- include the planned detail in the spoken version;
- label or explain the visual so it carries new information; and
- evaluate a peer's visual honestly against the "adds meaning" check.
