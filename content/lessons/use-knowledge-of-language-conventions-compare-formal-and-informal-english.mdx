## Lesson goal

Students notice that people adjust language for audience and purpose, then revise one message for a classroom setting.

## Prepare

- Display paired messages such as a quick playground invitation and a polite request to borrow a book.
- Prepare cards: **audience**, **purpose**, **words I choose**.
- Invite multilingual examples only when students volunteer them; do not ask children to perform a home language.

## Teaching sequence

1. Explain that language changes across friends, family, classroom, and written messages; it is not a contest between "good" and "bad" language.
2. Read two versions of a familiar request. Identify the audience and purpose for each.
3. Model revising a casual message into a clear, respectful classroom request.
4. Pairs choose one message card and make an audience-appropriate version through speech, writing, dictation, or AAC.
5. Share revisions and ask: "What choice helped the listener understand?"

## Print-ready audience card

| My audience | My purpose | Words I chose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| __________ | __________ | __________ |

## Access and observation

- Accept dialects and home languages as language resources; focus feedback on clarity for the chosen audience.
- Supply sentence starters and visual audience cards.
- Notice whether a student can name an audience or make one purposeful language choice.
