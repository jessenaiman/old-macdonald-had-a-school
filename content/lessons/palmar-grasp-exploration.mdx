## Purpose

Children practise holding, releasing, and placing objects on purpose. Infants
work on the whole-hand (palmar) grasp and a deliberate release; toddlers move
on to stacking rings, which adds hand-eye coordination and finger control.
Fifteen minutes, one tray per child, no worksheet.

## Source-linked song use

Sing or hum these documented baby-bounce rhymes to pace the tray work — one
verse per turn keeps hands busy and transitions gentle:

- **A Smooth Road** — *Baby Bounce Nursery Rhymes* (booklet).
- **All The Fish** — Mid North Coast Library (NSW, Australia) – *Baby Bounce Nursery Rhymes* (booklet).
- **Animal Noises** — Mid North Coast Library (NSW, Australia) – *Baby Bounce Nursery Rhymes* (booklet).

Use only the printed source text; the steady bounce beat is what makes them
useful here. Do not invent new verses.

## Prep and materials

- One grasping tray per infant: two or three large soft blocks or rattles big enough to never fit fully in a mouth
- One nesting-ring set per toddler seat
- A wipeable mat under each tray
- Linked topic material: [OT Toolbox](https://www.theottoolbox.com/) fine-motor activity ideas for extending the week
- The linked **Open Shut Them** song version as an optional finger-play warm-up from the topic's linked materials

## Brief teaching sequence

1. **Warm up fingers (2 min).** Do a short finger-play together — the linked Open Shut Them song version works well for open/close hands before grasping.
2. **Infant grasp-and-release (5 min).** Offer one block at a time. When an infant grips, wait. Narrate both halves: "You picked it up... and you let it go!" Re-offer without taking the object away mid-grip.
3. **Toddler ring stacking (5 min).** Toddlers place rings on the stack while you sing one verse of a source rhyme above per ring. Accept any placement order; name colours as they land.
4. **Close (3 min).** Hands together for one last verse of a source song, then each child drops their objects into the basket — a final grasp-release rep that also cleans up.

## Participation and access choices

- An infant may bat at the block instead of gripping; the bat is the same skill arriving early.
- Offer objects with different diameters so small hands can succeed somewhere.
- A toddler may unstack and restack, stack with two hands, or hand rings to a peer to place.
- Skip the warm-up finger-play for children who find hand-over-hand prompts upsetting; model in the air beside them instead.

## Teacher observation

Notice whether the child:

- closes a whole hand around an object and holds it through a movement;
- releases on purpose rather than only by dropping;
- transfers an object from one hand to the other;
- lines up a ring hole before dropping it onto the post; and
- repeats a grasp attempt after a miss.
