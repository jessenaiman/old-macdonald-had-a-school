## Lesson goal

Students use a partner conversation routine to listen, take one turn, and respond to an idea.

## Resource discovery cues for review

Use these cues to locate or review resources. They are search/review prompts,
not claims that a particular resource has already been approved.

- A Grade 1 visual partner-talk routine with a speaker/listener role card.
- A child-friendly discussion-agreement chart focused on listening and taking turns.
- A think-pair-share routine with private wait time before partner sharing.
- An AAC-inclusive turn-taking support with `my turn`, `your turn`, `I heard`, and `I want to add` options.
- A multilingual or home-language partner-talk support for early elementary discussion.

## Prepare

- Display two blank agreement cards: **We listen to understand** and **We take turns**.
- Give each pair a small object to hold while speaking, or offer a gesture/AAC turn card.
- Choose a familiar, non-personal prompt such as: "What is one thing a classroom needs?"

## Teaching sequence

1. Explain that a conversation works when everyone can think, speak, and listen safely.
2. Model a short conversation with a volunteer. Deliberately interrupt once; invite children to name what made listening hard.
3. Co-write the two agreements in child-friendly language. Add a picture or gesture beside each.
4. Give private think time, then partner turns. The listener repeats one idea back before adding their own.
5. Bring the class together. Ask: "Which agreement helped your partner?" Add one observed example to the cards.

## Print-ready partner card

| Speaker | Listener |
| --- | --- |
| I can say: "I think ..." | I can say/sign/show: "I heard ..." |
| I can pass if I am not ready. | I can wait and look at the speaker. |

## Access and observation

- Accept pointing, drawing, home language, AAC, gesture, or a rehearsed sentence as a valid turn.
- Offer a side-by-side partner or adult-supported pair for children who need a lower-demand entry.
- Notice whether each student can enter, pause, listen, respond, or decline respectfully. Do not score length of speech.
