## Lesson goal

Students use a nearby word, picture, or familiar word part to make a sensible guess about an unknown word.

## Prepare

- Select a short classroom read-aloud excerpt with one unfamiliar but inferable word.
- Make three cards: **Look around**, **Say a guess**, **Check it**.
- Prepare a small "word detective" recording strip.

## Teaching sequence

1. Read one sentence aloud and stop at the target word. Say: "I do not know this word yet, so I will look for clues."
2. Underline a helpful nearby word, phrase, or picture detail. Name it as evidence, not proof.
3. Make a child-friendly tentative guess. Reread the whole sentence to check whether the guess makes sense.
4. Repeat one example together, then let pairs try a second sentence with the three cards.
5. Close by collecting useful detective moves, including "ask for help" when the context is not enough.

## Print-ready word detective strip

| Unknown word | A clue I noticed | My first guess | Does it fit when I reread? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| __________ | __________ | __________ | yes / not yet |

## Access and observation

- Read every sentence aloud; do not make independent decoding a prerequisite for vocabulary thinking.
- Let students point to a clue, draw it, or choose between two picture-supported guesses.
- Notice whether students refer to evidence in the sentence or picture rather than whether they guess immediately.
