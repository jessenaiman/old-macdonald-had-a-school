## Prepare

- Editorial teacher preparation: Open the linked video on the device that will be used in class. Check volume, access, and the exact point where you want to pause.
- Gather optional animal picture cards for cow, duck, pig, horse, sheep, and chicken. These are teacher-supplied supports, not downloads from this page.
- Arrange a seated circle or safe standing space. Offer a quieter sound-making option before beginning.
- Canonical lead: Miss Puddles, staff music and dance teacher. Canonical student companion: Maisy.

## Watch and discuss

These are editorial prompts for using Jesse's video as a teaching model.

- Pause after an animal appears: What do you notice? Can you point to the matching card?
- Pause before the animal sound: What might we hear next? Children may echo, gesture, or watch.
- After a movement cue: Which part can we do seated? Which part could be softer?
- Before replaying a familiar section: What should we listen for this time - the beat, the animal name, or the sound?

## Play together

This is an editorial classroom sequence built from the authoritative queue's action notes; it is not a reproduction of the song text.

1. Invite children to look at the animal cards and wonder which animals might be in the barnyard.
2. Play the video. Keep a gentle pat or clap on the beat.
3. Pause at an animal-sound turn. Invite children to echo, point to the matching card, or make a quiet version of the sound.
4. Replay one familiar section and vary the volume: soft, then comfortable speaking or singing volume.
5. Finish by choosing one animal to move like, then return to stillness together.

## Follow up

- Editorial follow-up: Use teacher-made animal cards for a sound-and-picture matching activity.
- Invite children to choose one animal and show its sound with voice, gesture, or drawing.
- Keep the follow-up short: one match, one movement, and one return to the beat are enough for a between-lessons plan.

## Notice

- Observable learning: The child joins the shared beat, attempts an animal sound or gesture, and connects an animal name with its sound.
- Look for recall: The child anticipates an animal-sound turn or identifies a repeated animal without prompting.
- Curriculum connection: Source mapping `CU#226` names describing people, places, things, and events with relevant details. The classroom talk-and-picture-card bridge is editorial; `CU#226` is an internal mapping ID, not a verified Ontario code.
- Curriculum connection: Source mapping `CU#248` names plant/animal structures that help survival. Treat that as an editorial extension only; this song directly supports animal vocabulary, imitation, and participation, not a claim that the song teaches survival structures.

## Adapt

- Younger or less verbal children: use two animal cards, point, clap, or make a gesture instead of producing a sound.
- Children who prefer lower stimulation: watch the video, use the cards, tap softly, or make a silent animal movement.
- Older or ready children: invite them to choose the next card, compare loud and soft sounds, or describe one animal with a relevant detail.
- Mobility access: keep the sequence seated with finger, hand, or shoulder movements; no standing movement is required.
