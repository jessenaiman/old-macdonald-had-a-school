## Lesson goal

Students add a drawing, labelled sketch, or other visual display that makes an oral description clearer for a listener.

## Curriculum standards

- Ontario A1; Ontario LA A1
- US Common Core SL.1.5 (add drawings or other visual displays to descriptions when appropriate to clarify ideas, thoughts, and feelings)

## Prepare

- Show two prepared examples of the same familiar object: one with a useful labelled drawing, one with decoration only.
- Provide paper divided into two zones: **words** and **visual**.
- Gather pencils, crayons, label strips, and optional picture symbols.

## Teaching sequence

1. **Compare the examples (5 minutes).** Read both descriptions aloud. Ask which visual helps you learn something new — and why the other one does not.
2. **Model one informative mark (5 minutes).** Add a single label or arrow that explains a detail, saying aloud why it earns its place on the page.
3. **Students describe and draw (15 minutes).** Each student picks something they know well (a pet, a favourite spot, this classroom), describes it orally first, then adds a visual that clarifies one part.
4. **Partner check (5 minutes).** Using [Think-Pair-Share](https://www.readingrockets.org/classroom-classroom/strategies-routines/think-pair-share), partners say one new thing the visual taught them. If the partner learned nothing new, the artist knows to revise.
5. **Revise and share (5 minutes).** Students add, remove, or relabel one part so the visual communicates more, then a few volunteers present.

## Access and observation

- Offer stamps, photos, cut-outs, assistive drawing tools, or digital annotation instead of freehand drawing.
- Students may dictate their description while an adult scribes, then own the visual themselves.
- Notice whether each visual adds a relevant detail rather than decorating the page — that distinction is the standard.
- Graduated participation: choosing between two prepared labels counts as authoring the visual.
