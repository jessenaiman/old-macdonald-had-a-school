## Lesson goal

Children take part in a welcoming circle by noticing a peer's name and choosing a way to respond: voice, gesture, sign, picture choice, or watching.

## Materials

- Each child's name card, with a photo if available
- A floor spot or chair choice for each child
- A familiar shared object, such as a soft farm animal or class puppet
- Kathy Reid-Naiman's **Welcome Here** as the opening routine; use the licensed recording or your permitted classroom copy
- Optional free teacher choices: [Super Simple Songs](https://supersimple.com/super-simple-songs/), [Laurie Berkner videos](https://laurieberkner.com/watch), or [The Okee Dokee Brothers music and videos](https://www.okeedokee.org/music)

## Printable: name and greeting choice sheet

Print one copy and place it where children can point to a choice.

| When my name is heard, I can... | Choice |
| --- | --- |
| Say hello | voice |
| Wave | gesture |
| Touch my name card | picture or print |
| Use a sign or device | communication |
| Watch and listen | observation |

## Teaching sequence

1. **Welcome at the door.** Offer each child their name card and a choice of floor spot or chair. Do not require a spoken greeting.
2. **Open the circle.** Use *Welcome Here* as a short predictable greeting. Model one response choice without asking children to copy it.
3. **Share names slowly.** Hold up one name card at a time. Say the name once, pause, and invite the child to choose a response from the printable sheet.
4. **Build community language.** After the child responds, the group practices one brief shared response such as a wave or quiet hello. Keep the pace calm enough for every child to be noticed.
5. **Close with a transition.** Invite children to carry or place their name card at the next activity. Preview when the class will gather again.

## Participation and adaptation

- A child may watch the entire routine and still be participating.
- Use photos, home-language greetings, sign, AAC, or a family-provided pronunciation cue where helpful.
- Offer a quiet nearby seat, a trusted adult, or a held object for children who are not ready for the main circle.
- Repeat the same opening routine daily during the first weeks; change only one element at a time.

## Teacher observation

Notice whether the child:

- orients toward their name, name card, or a familiar adult;
- makes a chosen response by voice, movement, sign, device, or looking;
- notices a peer's turn; and
- returns to the routine with increasing comfort over several days.

## Teacher notes

The external options are choices for the teacher, not required viewing. Use one short resource at a time and keep the class's own name routine as the centre of the lesson.
