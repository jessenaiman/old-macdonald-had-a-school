## Lesson goal

Students pause during a read-aloud or short media text to ask and answer a question about an important detail, returning to what was heard or seen when explaining an answer.

## Curriculum standards

- Ontario A1; Ontario A1 / C2; Ontario C2
- US Common Core RI.1.1 (ask and answer questions about key details in a text)
- US Common Core SL.1.2 (ask and answer questions about key details from a text read aloud or information presented via other media)

## Prepare

- Choose one brief read-aloud excerpt or a short, school-approved media clip ([ReadWorks](https://www.readworks.org/) passages or a [Storyline Online](https://www.storylineonline.net/) video both work).
- Make four question-stem cards: **Who? What? Where? How do you know?**
- Post a two-column chart: **Our question** / **The part that answered it**.
- Note the timestamp or page of two details you can return to quickly.

## Teaching sequence

1. **Preview the stems (2 minutes).** Read the four cards aloud and explain that strong answers can point back to the exact place in the story or clip.
2. **First pass for enjoyment.** Read or play the text once without stopping.
3. **Model one question-and-answer (5 minutes).** Ask a Who? question, answer it, then rewind or turn back so students see the detail that proves the answer.
4. **Pair practice (12 minutes).** Pairs choose or generate one question stem, then reread or replay the relevant section to find the answer together.
5. **Share two examples (6 minutes).** Name the *detail* each pair found rather than praising speed alone; ask the class, "Which page or moment showed that?"

## Print-ready question card

| Our question | The page, picture, or part that helped | Our answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| __________ | __________ | __________ |

## Access and observation

- Offer question cards with icons and let students answer by pointing, drawing, acting, signing, or speaking.
- Provide the exact page or replay the frame for students who need memory support.
- Notice whether a student returns to what was heard or seen when explaining an answer — first with prompting, then on their own.
- Graduated participation: holding the card, pressing pause, or pointing counts as a full role.
