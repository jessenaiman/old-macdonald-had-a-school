## Purpose

One short circle where every child hears their own name in song and chooses
how to answer. Hearing names builds receptive language; answering — any way at
all — builds expressive language. Run it daily; by October it will run itself.

## Source-linked song use

- **Say Hello To The Morning** — Kathy Reid-Naiman (linked topic material; available on Spotify). Use as the recorded opener if your program permits streamed audio; otherwise sing the greeting live.
- **A Smooth Road** — *Baby Bounce Nursery Rhymes* (booklet); use its steady bounce verse as the calm-down close.
- **All The Fish** — Mid North Coast Library (NSW, Australia) – *Baby Bounce Nursery Rhymes* (booklet).
- **Animal Noises** — Mid North Coast Library (NSW, Australia) – *Baby Bounce Nursery Rhymes* (booklet); its call-and-answer animal sounds make a natural name-game bridge.

Insert each child's name into the booklet rhyme you know best rather than
rewriting lyrics on the spot — swap only the name slot, keep the printed text
otherwise as written.

## Prep and materials

- One photo name card per child (photo on front, name on back)
- A basket for the cards so children can draw them
- Your permitted copy/stream of *Say Hello To The Morning* – Kathy Reid-Naiman (Spotify), if using recorded music

## Brief teaching sequence

1. **Open identically every day (1 min).** Same seat arrangement, same first line of the greeting song. Predictability is what lets new toddlers join.
2. **Sing each name once (5 min).** Hold up one card, sing the child's name in the rhyme's melody, pause three full seconds. Accept any response as a completed turn: saying the name, waving, signing, touching the card, or smiling at a friend who answers.
3. **Echo peer names (2 min).** After a child responds, invite the group to echo the name once. Hearing peers' names is the point — do not correct pronunciation, just repeat it warmly and correctly yourself.
4. **Close with a booklet rhyme (2 min).** Sing one verse of a source-listed song above as the settle-down signal, then move to the next part of the day.

## Participation and access choices

- Watching quietly is participation; note it and move on without prompting a second time.
- Home-language greetings count fully — ask families for one greeting word and use it for that child.
- A child may respond through an AAC device, sign, or a familiar adult voicing for them.
- Keep the circle under ten minutes; two shorter circles beat one long one for this age.

## Teacher observation

Notice whether the child:

- orients toward their name when it is sung;
- produces a response within the wait time;
- attempts their own name or a peer's name in any form;
- joins the group echo by sound, movement, or attention; and
- anticipates the closing rhyme's ending.

## Printable/adaptable option: greeting-choice strip

Print one strip per child for families to mark how their child says hello at
home, so the room greets match home greetings.
