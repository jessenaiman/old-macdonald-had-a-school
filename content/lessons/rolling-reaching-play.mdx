## Purpose

In one short floor session, children practise moving their own bodies toward
something they want. Infants roll to reach a toy placed just past arm's length;
toddlers and two-year-olds roll back and forth on a mat with a grown-up's help.
The goal is effort and coordination, not distance or speed.

## Source-linked song use

Keep the session calm by humming or singing the documented baby-bounce songs as
background rhythm while children move:

- **A Smooth Road** — *Baby Bounce Nursery Rhymes* (booklet).
- **All The Fish** — Mid North Coast Library (NSW, Australia) – *Baby Bounce Nursery Rhymes* (booklet).
- **Animal Noises** — Mid North Coast Library (NSW, Australia) – *Baby Bounce Nursery Rhymes* (booklet).

Use the booklet texts as written; these are lap-bounce and action rhymes, so
the gentle beat pairs naturally with reaching and rolling. Do not add verses
that are not in the printed source.

## Prep and materials

- A clean floor mat or rug per child, with room to roll safely
- One motivating toy per infant, wiped down before use
- A low shelf or basket so toddlers can choose their own toy to roll toward
- Optional free teacher choice: [GoNoodle](https://www.gonoodle.com/) movement videos for the older children's warm-up

## Brief teaching sequence

1. **Set the stage (2 min).** Lay out mats with space between them. Place each infant on their tummy or back with a favourite toy just beyond reach.
2. **Narrate the reach (5 min).** As an infant rocks, pivots, or rolls toward the toy, describe it: "You rolled! Now your hand is almost there." Re-position the toy only when the child pauses.
3. **Roll with the older group (5 min).** Toddlers lie on a mat and log-roll from one end to the other while you sing one of the source-listed rhymes above. Offer a hand or hip-tap for children who cannot yet start the roll.
4. **Close calmly (3 min).** Return to sitting. Sing one final verse of a source song softly, collect toys together, and name what comes next in the day.

## Participation and access choices

- An infant who watches the toy without rolling is still working; hold the toy where their gaze can track it.
- Offer side-lying as a halfway option for children not yet rolling.
- A toddler may roll halfway, be rolled gently by a trusted adult, or simply lie still and feel the rhyme's beat.
- Keep any screen use (GoNoodle) teacher-facing: model the movement yourself rather than seating children in front of it.

## Teacher observation

Notice whether the child:

- reaches, pivots, or rolls toward a visible goal;
- repeats a successful movement without prompting;
- tolerates tummy time or mat time a little longer than last week;
- coordinates both sides of the body during the roll; and
- settles when the closing song signals the end.
