## Lesson goal

Students compare young animals with adults across a life cycle, describe one visible change from young to adult, and give one example of how an adult helps its offspring survive.

## Prepare

- Choose ONE short text or image sequence showing a familiar animal growing: the classroom copy of *The Very Hungry Caterpillar* (listed topic material) works well, or any labelled photo set of one animal at two or more stages.
- Post three sentence starters: **A baby ___ looks ___.**, **As it grows it ___.**, **The grown-up helps by ___.**
- Copy the observation table below, one per pair. No other prep needed.

## Teaching sequence

1. **Name the science job.** Tell students: "Today we are scientists comparing young animals and their adults. We are looking for what changes and what stays the same."
2. **Read or show the sequence once.** Pause at each stage. Ask only: "What do you notice about the young animal here?"
3. **Model one comparison.** Complete the first row of the table aloud for the class animal, thinking out loud: "The caterpillar has no wings; the butterfly has wings. That is a change."
4. **Pairs complete the table.** Each pair picks a second animal (from your photo set or the read-aloud) and fills in one change plus one way the adult helps — feeding, protecting, or teaching.
5. **Share and close.** Two pairs report one row each. Record their sentences on the board under "changes" and "how adults help," then preview tomorrow's living-things work.

## Access and graduated participation

- Students may point to matching photos, place stage cards in order, draw instead of write, or say their sentence to an adult who scribes it.
- Offer the sentence starters on the table itself so no student must hold them in memory.
- Accept a gesture sequence, an AAC message, or a partner-voiced answer as full participation in the share.
- Keep the song an invitation: listening is belonging.

## Teacher observation

Notice whether students:

- name a real difference between young and adult stages;
- connect the adult's action (feed, protect, carry) to the young animal surviving;
- return to the text or images when explaining an answer; and
- use science words such as *grow*, *change*, *adult*, and *offspring* in speech, writing, or AAC.
