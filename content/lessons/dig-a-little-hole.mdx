## Prepare

- Editorial teacher preparation: Preview the selected video on the classroom device and confirm sound, access, and the point where you want to pause.
- Place a small pot, soil, and a seed where children can see them, or use picture cards showing hole, seed, soil, water, and growing plant.
- Clear a small seated or standing movement space. A green ribbon or hand gesture can represent growing, but no special material is required.
- Canonical lead: Mr Sam, staff music and movement teacher. Canonical student companion: Whiskers.

## Watch and discuss

These are editorial prompts for using Jesse's video as a teaching model.

- Pause when the digging action begins: How could we show digging with just our fingers?
- Pause after the seed is placed: What comes next in our picture sequence?
- Pause at the growing visual: What changed from the seed? Show it with your body, hands, or eyes.
- Before replaying a short section: Should we watch for the beat, the action order, or the growing shape?

## Play together

This is an editorial classroom sequence built from the authoritative queue's action notes; it is not a reproduction of the song text.

1. Show a seed or the first picture card. Ask what a seed might need, accepting gestures and home-language responses.
2. Play the selected video. Move slowly through dig, place, cover, water, and grow.
3. Replay the sequence with children choosing seated hand motions, whole-body motions, or a growing motion.
4. Invite the group to make the growing motion taller while keeping the beat gentle and steady.
5. Put the picture cards in order together, then return to a calm resting shape.

## Follow up

- Editorial follow-up: Use a teacher-made four-step card sequence for children to order, point to, or act out.
- If appropriate, plant a real seed or observe a classroom plant over time; the observation is the follow-up, not a claim that the song is a complete science lesson.
- Invite children to draw one stage and name one thing a plant needs.

## Notice

- Observable learning: The child joins at least one planting action and follows part of the shared sequence.
- Look for recall: The child places or points to two or more steps in order, or uses planting words such as seed, soil, water, or grow.
- Curriculum connection: Source mapping `CU#251` names environmental plant needs as the primary Grade 2 mapping. The seed-to-plant classroom bridge is editorial and should not be presented as a verified Ontario code connection.
- Curriculum connection: Source mappings `CU#248`, `CU#252`, and `CU#253` suggest animal structures, seed dispersal and pollination, and habitats/biodiversity. These are editorial extensions for later inquiry; the song alone does not establish those concepts.

## Adapt

- Younger or less verbal children: focus on two contrasting actions, dig and grow, with large picture cues.
- Children with limited mobility: complete the sequence with finger or wrist movements, eye gaze to cards, or a partner modelling the actions.
- Sensory-sensitive children: skip real soil and use quiet playback, picture cards, and gentle gestures if needed.
- Older or ready children: ask what a seed needs, compare the order of the cards, or observe a real seed without claiming that the song is a complete science lesson.
