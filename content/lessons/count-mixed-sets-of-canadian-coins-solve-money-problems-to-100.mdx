## Lesson goal

Students sort a mixed set of Canadian coins by value, count the total using an organized strategy (for example all nickels first), and solve one money problem to 100¢.

## Prepare

- One mixed bag of plastic or real Canadian coins per pair: include nickels, dimes, quarters, loonies, and toonies. Keep totals under 100¢ for the main task.
- Printable coin mats from K5 Learning's Canadian money pages work as the copy-ready support (listed topic material) — print one per pair if you do not have enough coins.
- Post two strategy prompts: **First I sorted by ___.**, **I counted ___ because ___.**

## Teaching sequence

1. **Name the math job.** "Today we count like store clerks: organized first, fast second." Show an unsorted handful and ask what a clerk would do first.
2. **Sort before counting.** Pairs sort their bag by coin value onto the mat. Ask each pair to say their sorting rule in one sentence.
3. **Count with a strategy.** Pairs total their set, grouping same coins and skip-counting (5s, 10s, 25s). Record the total on the sheet.
4. **Compare strategies.** Two pairs share: same total? different order? Highlight that organizing by value — not by size or colour — makes counting reliable. Note that a toonie is worth more than a quarter even though it is bigger.
5. **Solve for a purpose.** Pose one classroom problem: "Our class needs 75¢ of craft supplies. Can your set pay for it exactly? What combination works?" Pairs answer with their counted set, then re-bag the coins.

## Access and graduated participation

- Students may sort by coin pictures on a mat before handling coins, use a hundreds chart to track counts, or state totals orally while a partner records.
- Accept pointing-based grouping with a spoken or AAC strategy sentence.
- Offer larger-print coin images for students who need contrast; keep real small coins supervised.
- Pair talk before whole-class shares so every student rehearses one strategy sentence.

## Teacher observation

Notice whether students:

- sort by value rather than size or colour;
- skip-count accurately within one group before combining groups;
- explain WHY their order is efficient; and
- connect the 75¢ problem back to their actual counted set.
