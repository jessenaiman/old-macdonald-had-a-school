## Lesson goal

Students listen to a short talk by a classmate or adult, restate one idea in their own words, and ask a question that clarifies or extends what was said.

## Prepare

- Line up one short speaker: a 60–90 second classmate show-and-tell, or a recorded read-aloud from Storyline Online (listed topic material).
- Post question stems where all can see them: **I heard ...**, **Can you tell me more about ...?**, **What did you mean when you ...?**
- Copy the listening card below, one per student. That is the whole prep.

## Teaching sequence

1. **Set the listening job.** Tell students: "Your job is one idea, not every word. First we restate it; then we ask about it."
2. **Listen once.** The speaker talks for up to 90 seconds. Hold all questions during the talk — thinking time first.
3. **Model a restatement.** Say: "I heard that ___. Did I get it right?" Let the speaker confirm or correct — correcting the listener is a real speaking job too.
4. **Partner rounds.** In pairs, each student restates one idea and picks or writes one question stem for the speaker. Two pairs then ask their questions aloud; the class sorts them into "already answered" and "helps us learn more."
5. **Close with a norm.** Ask students to name one thing a good question-listener does (wait, look at the speaker, build on the answer). Keep it on an anchor chart for tomorrow.

## Access and graduated participation

- Students may choose between two printed question stems before generating their own.
- Accept a drawn restatement with a spoken caption, or an AAC message as the restatement.
- Permit a partner to co-read the card aloud; the thinking is the work, not solo reading speed.
- Offer the speaker role with supports too: photo cues, extra wait time, or a rehearsed first line.

## Teacher observation

Notice whether students:

- restate an idea that was actually said rather than a new topic;
- connect their question to the restatement;
- wait for the speaker to finish before asking; and
- respond to the answer instead of repeating the same question.
