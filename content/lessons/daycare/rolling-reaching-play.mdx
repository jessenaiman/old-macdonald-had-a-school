## Lesson goal

Children have repeated opportunities to roll, reach, track a nearby object, and choose how they participate in safe floor play.

## Materials

- Clean floor mats or a clear, soft floor area
- A few large, safe, easy-to-grasp toys placed just beyond a child's comfortable reach
- A basket for returning toys between turns
- Kathy Reid-Naiman's **We're Jumping** as a brief upright movement cue before floor play; use the teacher's permitted classroom copy
- The linked **Roll, Roll, Roll Your Hands** movement cue as an optional seated or standing warm-up

## Teaching sequence

1. **Invite, do not require.** Gather for a very short upright cue. Children may move, watch, or join one counted jump.
2. **Set up the floor choice.** Place one toy just beyond a child's current reach. Name what you notice rather than directing a particular movement: “The ball is close by.”
3. **Give time.** Allow the child to reach, turn, roll, track the toy with their eyes, or choose not to move. Return the toy slowly so the child can try again.
4. **Repeat with variation.** Change only one detail at a time: the toy, the side, or the distance. Older children may roll back and forth on a mat with close adult support.
5. **Close calmly.** Use the hand-rolling cue or a quiet return-to-basket routine, then transition to the next part of the day.

## Participation and adaptation

- Watching a peer, looking toward a toy, or reaching from a supported position are valid ways to participate.
- Use a familiar toy, a caregiver-supported position, or more space around the child when that helps them join comfortably.
- Keep rolling and reaching as the main experience. The songs are optional entry cues, not the goal of the lesson.
- Pause or adjust when a child signals discomfort, fatigue, or a wish to stop.

## Teacher observation

Notice whether the child:

- turns or looks toward a toy placed nearby;
- reaches, shifts weight, rolls, or repeats an attempted movement;
- tracks the toy or a peer's movement; and
- shows a preferred way to enter or leave the floor-play routine.
