## Purpose

Use the same short song-and-touch routine during September arrival, after a
care transition, or before rest so each child can experience a predictable,
responsive connection with a familiar adult. The purpose is relationship and
routine, not group performance or teaching the words.

## Source-linked song use

Use Kathy Reid-Naiman's **A Smooth Road** from the existing linked song record.
The database record supplies the sequence of gentle swaying, gentle bounces,
big bounces, and a gentle, quick lowering at the final cue. Use only a
permitted classroom copy. The local public-library version provides a visible
comparison source for the same named movement pattern; it is not evidence that
the Kathy Reid-Naiman recording is freely available.

## Prep and materials

- A consistent chair, floor cushion, or safe standing space for each adult-child pair
- A familiar comfort item if the child uses one
- A simple visual card showing `smooth`, `bumpy`, `rough`, and `all done`
- The teacher's permitted classroom copy of the selected song, if used

## Brief teaching sequence

1. **Connect first.** Greet the child by name, come to their level, and wait for a look, reach, vocalization, body shift, or other sign that tells you how close to be.
2. **Offer the routine.** Say or show the visual: "Road song?" A child may accept a lap version, choose a standing sway with the adult, hold a toy while listening, or decline.
3. **Follow the documented pattern.** Use the song's gentle sway/bounce progression only at an intensity the child accepts. Pause between changes so the child can signal `more`, `again`, `stop`, or `all done`.
4. **Name the transition.** End with the same brief phrase, such as "Road song finished. Now we [wash hands / play / rest]." Keep the next step visible and calm.
5. **Repeat rather than extend.** Offer the same short routine over several days; do not lengthen it to fill a group circle.

## Participation and access choices

- Listening, watching the adult's face, holding a hand, rocking independently, or declining are all participation.
- Do not bounce a child who resists, is distressed, is medically unable to participate, or whose caregiver plan does not support it.
- Use a seated sway, a hand-on-heart pulse, a scarf moving over the adult's own hand, or no movement when lap play is not appropriate.
- Ask families which songs, words, and soothing routines matter at home; use their guidance rather than substituting an assumed cultural routine.

## Teacher observation

Notice whether the child:

- anticipates a familiar part of the routine with gaze, body movement, or sound;
- signals a preference for closeness, movement, a comfort item, or stopping;
- returns attention to the adult after a pause; and
- settles into or transitions out of the routine with adult support.

## Printable: caregiver routine card

Print and place near the arrival/rest area.

| Before | During | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Get low, greet by name, and offer a choice. | Match movement to the child's cues; pause for a response. | Name what comes next and keep the transition predictable. |
