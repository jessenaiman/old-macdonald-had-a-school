## Purpose

Practise the four routines that carry every September day: beginning together,
stopping on a cue, listening while a peer speaks, and waiting for what comes
next. The lesson rehearses cues inside real transitions so the practice costs
no extra teaching time.

## Source-linked song use

- **All The Fish** — Mid North Coast Library (NSW, Australia) – *Baby Bounce Nursery Rhymes* (booklet). Its repeated action verses make it a ready-made stop/start game: sing a verse, hands still on the pause, sing again.
- The linked topic material **Open Shut Them** works as a fingers-ready warm-up before circle.

Use only the printed source text from the booklet; do not invent new verses as
if they came from the source — say added actions plainly yourself instead.

## Prep and materials

- A visual schedule strip showing today's first three activities
- Two teacher-held cue cards or objects: one for `start`, one for `stop` (a chime or hand signal works equally well)
- One real transition to practise (toys to carpet, carpet to line-up)
- Linked topic material: [Pathway 2 Success](https://www.thepathway2success.com/) routines and expectations resources for extending the week
- Your permitted classroom copy of any recorded song you choose to use

## Brief teaching sequence

1. **Name the cues (3 min).** Show both cards. Demonstrate each once yourself. State plainly: "When the stop card comes up, our hands and feet are still. Then we wait."
2. **Play it with the song (5 min).** Sing **All The Fish** verse by verse. Children do the printed action during the verse and freeze at your stop card. Repeat until the freeze needs no reminder — that is the success measure, not silence.
3. **Rehearse one real transition (7 min).** Use the same two cards for an actual move: start cue → children begin tidying; stop cue → everyone stills; you give the next instruction only after the stop. Narrate the group's success specifically: "You stopped on the first card."
4. **Reflect briefly (3 min).** Ask children what their body does on the stop cue. Accept words, gestures, or pointing at the card.
5. **Close (2 min).** Preview tomorrow using the visual schedule and repeat the start cue once more as the send-off.

## Participation and access choices

- A child may freeze hands-only, sit near the edge, hold the stop card, or watch a peer to know when to stop — all valid routes to the same routine.
- Pair the verbal cue with the visual card permanently; fade neither yet.
- Children who need movement can be the `start` leader who shows the first action.
- Offer a home-language translation of `start` and `stop` from families and add it beside the cards.

## Teacher observation

Notice whether the child:

- begins an activity after the start cue without an individual reminder;
- stops within a few seconds of the stop cue;
- waits for the next instruction rather than moving early;
- reminds or models the cue for a peer kindly; and
- uses the visual schedule to predict the next step.

## Printable/adaptable option: routine cue poster

Print one poster per routine spot (carpet, coat hooks, washroom door).

| Cue | What my body does | Who can help me |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Start | Begin the job / join the circle | Any educator |
| Stop | Hands still, feet still, ears ready | Educator on duty |
