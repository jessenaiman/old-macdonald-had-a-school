## Purpose

Children name — in whatever medium suits them — the people who care for them.
Families look different in every home; the lesson uses `caring network`
language so grandparents, foster families, two homes, and chosen helpers all
fit without explanation. Sharing stays optional from start to finish.

## Source-linked song use

- **All The Fish** — Mid North Coast Library (NSW, Australia) – *Baby Bounce Nursery Rhymes* (booklet). Use its repeated action verse as the circle opener; its call-and-response rhythm also carries the optional share turn nicely.
- The linked topic material **Open Shut Them** is a calm hands-ready warm-up before children draw.

Stick to the printed booklet text. If you adapt a verse to name family members,
present it as your class's own version, not as the source.

## Prep and materials

- One blank `My Caring Network` page per child: a large house outline or circle map with room for names or pictures
- Crayons, pencils, photo cut-outs, and glue sticks so drawing is never the only option
- A quiet side table for children who prefer to build rather than share
- Optional: family photos collected beforehand only where families chose to send them

## Brief teaching sequence

1. **Open the circle (3 min).** Sing the source song opener (**All The Fish**). Frame the work plainly: "Every person has people who care for them. Today we make a picture of our people."
2. **Model with your own network (2 min).** Fill in your own caring-network page first — including a friend and a neighbour, not just family — so many shapes of care are visible from minute one.
3. **Create (10 min).** Children add people by drawing, gluing photos, writing names, using stamps for each person, or telling an educator who scribes verbatim. No minimum number; one name counts.
4. **Optional share (3 min).** Using the song's call-and-response beat, invite anyone who wants to show one person and say one thing: "This is my Oma. She walks me to school." Passing is a complete answer; move on warmly.
5. **Close (2 min).** Pages go home or onto a display wall only if the child chooses. Sing one last verse together and preview tomorrow.

## Participation and access choices

- A child may keep their page private, share it with one friend instead of the group, or leave a section blank.
- AAC users may pre-record or type one sentence about a person on their page.
- Offer scribing for every child so writing ability never limits what they can express.
- Send a family note offering a word in the home language for `family` or `grandparent`, and use it on the child's page if they wish.

## Teacher observation

Notice whether the child:

- identifies at least one caring person by name, image, or gesture;
- represents people with words, images, marks, or movement;
- chooses to share, pass, or share privately — and owns that choice;
- responds kindly when a peer's family looks different from theirs; and
- uses family vocabulary in either home language or English during play afterwards.

## Printable/adaptable option: My Caring Network page

Print one per child; the same page works for draw, glue, stamp, or scribe.

| This person cares for me by... | Name or picture here | One thing we do together |
| --- | --- | --- |
| (repeat rows as needed) | | |
