## Lesson goal

Students describe one familiar object, place, person, or event with two relevant details.

## Prepare

- Place a familiar object in a bag or use a detailed classroom photo.
- Display detail prompts: **looks like**, **feels/sounds like**, **where/when**, **what it does**.
- Prepare a simple sketch box.

## Teaching sequence

1. Reveal the object or image and name the difference between a detail and a guess.
2. Model a two-detail description without saying the item name. Let students infer it.
3. Gather details as a class, sorting useful details from unrelated ones.
4. Students choose an item/photo and draw or label two details before telling a partner.
5. Partners name one detail that helped them picture it. Revise one description together.

## Print-ready detail planner

## Access and observation

- Offer real objects, tactile materials, photos, voice recording, dictation, or AAC descriptions.
- Let students describe from a known experience rather than demanding a novel topic.
- Notice whether a detail helps a listener identify or understand the subject.
