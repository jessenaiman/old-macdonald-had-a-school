## Lesson goal

Students read words containing common vowel-team spellings, sort the words by team, and spell two new words using a pattern they already know.

## Prepare

- Choose 8–10 decodable words with vowel teams your class has met (for example: *rain*, *play*, *team*, *boat*, *book*, *food*), drawn from your linked topic materials (Reading Universe; UFLI Foundations).
- Copy the sort card below or write the word list on the board in mixed order.
- Have whiteboards or paper for quick spelling tries. No other materials needed.

## Teaching sequence

1. **Warm up with sound, not letters.** Say three review words; students signal the vowel sound they hear (long or other) with a hand sign.
2. **Model one word.** Write *rain*. Underline **ai** and think aloud: "Two letters, one sound — that is a vowel team working together."
3. **Choral read the set once.** Point to each word; all students read together so no one is put on the spot first.
4. **Partner sort.** Pairs sort the words into columns by vowel team (**ai**, **oa**, **ee**, ...). Circulate and ask, "How do you know these go together?"
5. **Spell to transfer.** Say one NEW word that uses a sorted team (*pail*, *coat*, *seed*). Students write it, then check it against the sorted column. Close by asking one pair to name a pattern they could use on a brand-new word tomorrow.

## Print-ready vowel-team sort card

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| rain | boat | seed | |

Replace the header teams and example words with the set your class has actually met.

## Access and graduated participation

- Students may read chorally before reading alone, tap the vowel team instead of underlining, or sort picture-supported cards.
- Accept pointing-based sorting with a partner voicing the reason.
- Offer letter tiles or an AAC/keyboard option for the spelling try.
- A student who listens and holds the sort cards is still doing phonics work.

## Teacher observation

Notice whether students:

- identify the two-letter team inside a whole word rather than guessing from the first letter;
- explain why two words share a column;
- transfer a known team to spell a new word; and
- self-correct when a tried spelling does not look right against the sort.
