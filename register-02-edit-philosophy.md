# Chromatic Kinship

The visual language is tactile order: warm paper, stitched edges, rounded forms, and a quiet grid that lets relationships read before labels do. Every character belongs to a family, and every family is made visible through repeated circles, cards, and paths.

Color carries the meaning. Eight teacher anchors hold the structure; eight learner tints appear as the same hue lifted toward morning light. The palette is calibrated, not decorative, and every surface should feel materially connected to the same felt-and-paper world.

Scale creates welcome. Large character figures establish recognition, while smaller patches, badges, and labels create rhythm and invite closer looking. The compositions should breathe at the margins and remain calm even when information is dense.

The finish is meticulously crafted: stitched borders, soft shadows, paper grain, and carefully aligned type should feel labored over by a master maker. Details are sparse but exact, with visual hierarchy doing the explanatory work and text remaining only where it anchors the scene.

The final impression is a coherent school family—distinct faces, one chromatic system, and a sense of patient, handmade care.
