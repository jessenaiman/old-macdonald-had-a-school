# Tactile Restraint

Tactile Restraint treats paper as a working material rather than a decorative pattern. Each sheet has a purpose made visible through proportion, edge character, fibre, and ruling. Empty space remains genuinely writable, with texture quiet enough to support interface copy instead of competing with it. The family should feel meticulously crafted and materially credible at every scale.

Form follows classroom use. Square reminders, narrow checklists, tall planning slips, wide teacher notes, compact mobile strips, and ruled reflection sheets differ because their content differs. The silhouettes stay simple and legible; small deckled variations provide evidence of the hand without becoming theatrical. Every ratio and corner is resolved with painstaking attention.

Colour stays within warm ivory, oat, pale manila, and restrained blue-grey ruling. Variation comes primarily from fibre density, edge treatment, and proportion rather than arbitrary colour. The result should feel authored by a master compositor who understands that readable paper is a neutral carrier for thought.

Scale creates the system. At a glance the family reads as one material language; up close, each piece reveals its own fine grain and edge. Shadows belong to the interface composition, not the source asset, so the paper remains reusable and clean. This separation is the product of deep implementation expertise as much as visual taste.

The final sheet is a specimen, not a collage. Objects sit on a strict transparent grid with generous isolation and no baked labels, pins, clips, shadows, or interface text. The craftsmanship lives in the controlled surfaces and consistent alpha edges, refined as though each asset had been prepared by hand over countless hours.
